Module 1: Two Doors of Funding

The most common mistake PVV members make is confusing money for the club with money for clients. They're completely different applications, different accounts, different rules.

Door 1 - PVV/Stanford Funds PVV operations: transportation, club events, printing. Applied for by our Treasurer through Haas or OSE.

Door 2 - Client nonprofits Funds the client's programs: staff, supplies, services. The nonprofit applies. PVV advises and helps write - we don't submit as Stanford.

Module 2: Budgets That Funders Trust

Funders don't fund vibes. They fund line items where every dollar has a job and the math adds up.

Budget categories

CategoryExamples
PersonnelPart-time coordinator, tutor stipends
Direct programFood, textbooks, supplies for clients
TransportationVan rental, mileage
AdminInsurance, office supplies (often capped at 10–15%)
In-kind matchVolunteer hours valued at $29.95/hr (Independent Sector rate)
Golden rule If your narrative says "serve 45 students twice weekly," your budget must show personnel hours and supplies for exactly that. Reviewers check.

Module 3: Grant Narratives

Most grants want the same things in different words: who you are, what problem exists, what you'll do, how you'll measure success, and what it costs.

SMART outcomes

BadGood
"Help students""80% of 30 students improve ≥1 grade level in math by June 2027"
"Reduce hunger""Serve 200 families/month with weekly groceries for 12 months"

Problem statement formula

Data: In [community], [X]% experience [problem].
Story: [One person, with permission].
Consequence: Without help, [what happens].
Your angle: [Org] is positioned to address this because [evidence].
