Module 1: Your role at PVV

About 8 minutes

PVV exists to connect local nonprofits and community organizations serving low-income communities with free, student-provided consulting. Marketing is one of three consulting tracks, alongside Financial and Technical.

Under PVV's constitution, marketing help means three things: creating and maintaining a client's online presence (social media), designing and distributing flyers, and promoting client events to Stanford students through the Haas Center for Public Service.

Module 2: One goal, one audience

About 10 minutes

Small nonprofits rarely struggle with marketing because they're bad at it. They struggle because they try to do everything at once. Your first job is to narrow the plan.

The ruleOne goal. One primary audience. One or two channels. Master those before adding anything else.
GoalMeasure it by
Event attendanceRSVPs or head count
Volunteer recruitmentSign-up form submissions
DonationsDollars raised or donate-link clicks
AwarenessFollowers, reach, or "how did you hear about us" answers

Two audiences, two messages

Most PVV campaigns speak to two groups. The first is the community the nonprofit serves, who find events on Instagram, Facebook, and flyers at laundromats and churches. The second is Stanford students who might attend or volunteer, who hear about things through Haas Center emails and Instagram. They need different words in different places.

Module 3: Message and story

About 10 minutes

People act when they understand what's happening and why it matters to someone. A key message gives every post, flyer, and email the same spine, so you aren't reinventing the pitch each time.

Key message formula[Organization] helps [who] by [how]. Right now, we need [specific ask] so that [impact].

Example: Eastside Community Pantry helps low-income families in East Palo Alto by providing free weekly groceries. Right now, we need you at our Taco Tuesday fundraiser on March 15 so that we can keep our shelves stocked through summer.

The caption formula

PartWhat it does
HookA question, a number, or a bold statement that stops the scroll
StoryTwo or three sentences on why this matters to a real person
DetailsDate, time, location, and where to find more
Call to actionOne clear next step, such as "Link in bio to RSVP"
Stories need permissionBefore you share a client's photo, name, or story, get their permission and the nonprofit's approval. When in doubt, tell the story without identifying details.

Module 4: Design a flyer people read

About 12 minutes

A stranger at a laundromat bulletin board gives your flyer about three seconds. In that time they should know what's happening, when, where, and what to do next. If any of those is missing, the flyer fails.

The squint testZoom out until the flyer is thumbnail-sized. Can you still read the event name and the date? If not, simplify.

Readable also means high contrast. WCAG, the standard for accessible design, asks for at least 4.5:1 contrast between normal-size text and its background, and 3:1 for large text. A free checker will tell you the ratio for any pair of colors.

Module 5: Channels and Stanford promotion

About 10 minutes

Pick one primary channel and one secondary channel, and do them well. Consistency beats volume: two or three good posts a week outperform daily filler.

Module 6: Measure and hand off

About 10 minutes

Posting is half the job. The other half is knowing whether it worked and leaving the client able to carry on without you.

Track thisIgnore this, for now
Volunteer form submissionsTotal follower count
Event attendanceLikes on unrelated posts
Donation link clicksImpressions with no action
"How did you hear about us?" answersCompetitors' follower counts

The Assigned Liaison

Each Bridge Kit has one PVV member as its Assigned Liaison for the quarter. The client approves copy once at kickoff, and the liaison handles posting, recruiting Stanford peers, and attending one client event. At the end of the quarter, the liaison writes a handoff note so the next person can pick up cleanly. The client keeps the logins.
