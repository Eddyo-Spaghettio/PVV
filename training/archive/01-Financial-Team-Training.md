## Module 0: Orientation - Two Money Universes
**XP: 50 · Time: 30 min**

### The Plot Twist Nobody Expects

Picture **Tony Stark** trying to pay for Avengers HQ repairs with **Peter Parker's allowance**. Chaos. Embarrassing emails. Possibly an audit.

PVV lives in **two separate financial universes**. Mixing them up is our #1 rookie mistake.

| | **Universe A: PVV (The Club)** | **Universe B: Client Nonprofits** |
|---|-------------------------------|-----------------------------------|
| **Who gets money?** | Proyecto Vidas Valiosas (student org) | Oak Street Pantry, real CBO partners |
| **Who applies?** | PVV Treasurer + officers | Client's ED/board (we assist) |
| **Typical sources** | Haas Center transportation grants, event co-sponsorship ($250–500), OSE/VSO co-sponsorships | Foundations, government grants, corporate giving |
| **501(c)(3) status?** | PVV is a **student organization**, not a nonprofit | Clients usually **are** 501(c)(3)s |
| **Example use** | Uber to volunteer day, printing club flyers | Pay pantry staff, buy food, expand programs |
| **PVV's role** | Officers apply & manage | **Consult:** research, draft, budget - client submits |

### ✅ Knowledge Check
1. Can PVV apply for a $50,000 USDA food security grant **for itself**? *(No - wrong universe unless the grant explicitly funds student org programs.)*
2. Can PVV **help Oak Street draft** that same grant? *(Yes - that's our purpose.)*
3. Who signs the final grant application for a client? *(Their authorized officer, not a PVV student.)*

## Module 1: The Budget Boss Battle
**XP: 100 · Time: 90 min**

### Story Hook: The $847 Mystery

Mari discovers OSCP spent **$847 more than planned** on refrigeration repairs. The board is panicking. Professor McBudget appears (in your imagination) and says: *"You cannot grant-write your way out of a budget you don't understand."*

### What Is a Nonprofit Budget?

A budget = **planned** income + **planned** expenses for a time period (usually 1 year, sometimes project-specific).

**Income categories (common):** - Grants & foundation funding - Individual donations - Corporate sponsorships - Earned income ( thrift store, fees - if any) - In-kind donations (count carefully - some grants ask you to exclude these)

**Expense categories (common):** - **Program costs** - directly serves mission (food, supplies, client services) - **Personnel** - salaries, benefits, payroll taxes - **Operations** - rent, utilities, insurance, office - **Fundraising** - events, donor mailings (some funders cap this %) - **Administrative** - accounting, legal, bank fees

### The Golden Rule of Grant Budgets

> **Funders pay for incremental costs tied to *their* project - not your whole org unless unrestricted.**

If the grant is "Weekend Pantry Expansion," budget **only** the new weekend hours: extra staff time, extra food, extra utilities - not your entire annual rent (unless the funder allows "indirect/overhead").

### Hands-On Lab: Build OSCP's Project Budget

**Scenario:** Oak Street wants **$15,000** to run expanded Saturday hours for 6 months.

Create a spreadsheet with these columns:

| Line Item | Category | Unit Cost | Quantity | Total | Notes |
|-----------|----------|-----------|----------|-------|-------|
| Part-time pantry coordinator (Sat) | Personnel | $22/hr | 8 hrs × 26 Saturdays | $4,576 | New role |
| Fresh produce | Program | $350/week | 26 weeks | $9,100 | 40% of budget |
| Refrigeration energy | Operations | $45/month | 6 months | $270 | Incremental |
| Volunteer appreciation | Program | $150/event | 4 events | $600 | Retention |
| **TOTAL** | | | | **$14,546** | Room for $454 contingency |

**Pro tips:** - Round sensibly; don't fake precision ($4,576.83 vibes = suspicious) - Add **10% contingency** line if allowed - Match narrative: if your proposal says "serve 200 families," food costs should reflect that

### Example: Budget Narrative Paragraph

> *"Funds will support a part-time Saturday coordinator ($4,576) and weekly fresh produce purchases ($9,100) to serve an estimated 200 additional households over 26 weeks. A modest contingency ($454) covers seasonal price spikes documented in our 2024 vendor receipts."*

**Grant hunting** is archaeology. You're digging through foundation websites hoping to find a treasure map that says: *"Must serve low-income communities in San Mateo County. Deadline: March 1."*

### The Grant Research Process

```
1. CLARIFY need → 2. SEARCH sources → 3. CHECK eligibility → 4. SCORE fit → 5. TRACK deadlines
```

### Where to Look (Client Grants)

| Source type | Examples | Best for |
|-------------|----------|----------|
| Community foundations | Silicon Valley Community Foundation, local CFs | Place-based orgs |
| Family foundations | (Google "[region] foundation grants") | Specific issue areas |
| Corporate giving | Safeway Foundation, local banks | Food, youth, housing |
| Government | City/county community grants, SAM.gov (federal) | Larger orgs with capacity |
| Aggregators | Instrumentl, Candid, Foundation Directory | Research (may need library access) |

### Eligibility Red Flags 🚩

Stop and read carefully: - **501(c)(3) required?** - Does client have IRS determination letter? - **Geography** - "Bay Area" vs "San Mateo County only" - **Budget ceiling** - "Orgs under $250K annual budget" - **Matching funds** - Must raise 1:1 match? - **Restrictions** - No religious activity, no lobbying, no capital campaigns

## Module 3: Writing the Proposal - The Oak Street Arc
**XP: 150 · Time: 2 hours**

### Story Hook: The Fellowship of the Ring, but It's a Google Doc

Grant writing is a **team quest**. Mari has the stories. You have structure. Together you produce something funders actually read (rare and beautiful).

### Standard Proposal Sections

Most grants ask variations of:

1. **Organization overview** - Who are you? Credibility.
2. **Statement of need** - What's the problem? Use **data + human story**.
3. **Project description** - What will you do? Methods, timeline.
4. **Goals & outcomes** - Measurable! ("Serve 200 households" not "help people")
5. **Budget & budget narrative** - Module 1 returns!
6. **Evaluation plan** - How will you know it worked?
7. **Sustainability** - What happens after the grant ends?

### The NEED Section Formula

```
DATA (1-2 sentences) + STORY (1 paragraph) + LOCAL CONTEXT (why you?)
```

**Example for OSCP:**

> *In East Palo Alto, 34% of households experience food insecurity (County Health Report, 2024). Last Tuesday, Rosa M. arrived at Oak Street at 4:47pm - twelve minutes after we closed - holding two grandchildren's hands. "I get off work at 4:30," she said. "Saturday is the only day I don't choose between rent and groceries." Oak Street has served this neighborhood for 11 years; our waitlist for weekend slots has tripled since 2023.*

See? Data + human + why **this org**.

### SMART Goals Template

| Goal | Metric | Target | Timeline |
|------|--------|--------|----------|
| Expand access | Households served Sat | +200 | 6 months |
| Nutrition | % fresh produce distributed | 60% | Ongoing |
| Retention | Volunteer return rate | 75% | 6 months |

### Hands-On Lab: Draft a 2-Page Mini-Proposal

Using your Module 1 budget and Module 2 top grant pick, write: - [ ] 150-word organization overview - [ ] 250-word statement of need (use Rosa or create your own character) - [ ] 300-word project description with timeline (bullet milestones OK) - [ ] 3 SMART goals - [ ] Budget summary table + 100-word budget narrative

**Voice tip:** Write in **client's voice** ("we," "our pantry"), not PVV's. Add header: *"DRAFT - Prepared with assistance from Proyecto Vidas Valiosas consulting team."*

### Common Mistakes (Villain List)

| Villain | What they do | How to defeat |
|---------|--------------|---------------|
| **Vague Von Verbosity** | "We empower holistic synergies" | Use concrete nouns: meals, families, hours |
| **Budget Drift** | Narrative says 500 families, budget feeds 50 | Reconcile numbers |
| **Deadline Denial** | Submits at 11:59:01 PM | Finish 48 hrs early for Mari to review |
| **Attachment Amnesia** | Forgets IRS letter | Checklist before submit |

### 🎯 Deliverable
2-page draft proposal for Oak Street (Google Doc). Highlight **[CLIENT TO COMPLETE]** anywhere you need real data.

## Module 4: Club Grants - Haas Center & Stanford
**XP: 100 · Time: 60 min**

### Story Hook: Funding Your Own Superhero HQ

Remember Universe A? PVV needs money too - mostly so you can **get to clients** and **host events** that help them.

### Haas Center: Transportation Grants

**Purpose:** Subsidize travel to public service activities  
**Amount:** Up to **$200/student** or **$500/SSO per quarter** (combined cap for group)  
**Reality check:** Funds are limited; re-apply each quarter; submit reflection on prior grant impact

**Eligible uses:** - Uber/Lyft to volunteer/consulting sites (public service rides only) - Personal car mileage reimbursement (specific process) - **Not:** Tips, Zipcar membership, non-service travel

**Application tips:** - ONE application per SSO per quarter - coordinate with Treasurer - Complete projected budget before applying - Reimbursement windows: Week 5 & Week 10 deadlines

**Contact:** Geoff Baker (Haas Center) - verify current process on [Haas Service Org Resources](https://haas.stanford.edu/resources/students/service-organization-resources)

### Haas Center: Event Co-Sponsorship

**Purpose:** Promote + sometimes fund public service events  
**Funding range:** Typically **$250–$500** (case-by-case)  
**Lead time:** **2 months** for funding requests; **2 weeks** for promo-only

**Requirements:** - Event relates to public service - Connects to Pathways of Public Service - Budget with revenues AND expenses submitted - Appropriate: food, honoraria, marketing materials - **Not:** partisan politics, individual conference attendance, field trips

**Promo support:** Haas website, social, newsletters - great for **client events** you help publicize!

## Module 5: Case Management & Donor Tracking
**XP: 100 · Time: 75 min**

### Story Hook: Hermione's Color-Coded Filing System

Grants aren't "submit and forget." Nonprofits need **case management** for: - Active grant reporting - Donor stewardship - Restricted vs unrestricted funds

### Restricted vs Unrestricted Money

| Type | Rule | Example |
|------|------|---------|
| **Restricted** | Must use for stated purpose | "Weekend food grant" → food only |
| **Unrestricted** | General operations | Small individual donations |
| **Temporarily restricted** | Time/program bound | Multi-year pledge |

**Consultant rule:** Always label restricted funds in budget templates. Mari should never accidentally pay rent with food-only grant money.

### Simple Grant Tracking Sheet (For Clients)

| Grant Name | Funder | Amount | Start | End | Report Due | Status | Restrictions |
|------------|--------|--------|-------|-----|------------|--------|--------------|
| Fresh Foods Fund | SVCF | $15,000 | 7/1/26 | 12/31/26 | 1/15/27 | Active | Food program only |

### Donation & Grant Pipeline (CRM-lite)

For small orgs, a Google Sheet beats an expensive CRM. Columns:

`Donor/Grant | Type | Stage (Prospect→Applied→Awarded→Report) | Amount | Next Action | Owner | Due Date`

### Reporting Basics

Most grants require **interim or final reports**: - Activities completed vs planned - Outcomes metrics - Financial accounting (every dollar) - Stories/photos (with permission!)

**PVV help:** Draft report templates before the deadline crunch.

### Hands-On Lab

Create for Oak Street:
1. Grant tracking sheet (1 active + 1 prospective grant)
2. 90-day calendar with report deadlines
3. Email template to funder: *"Quarterly progress update"*

Mari Santos has 20 minutes with a foundation program officer (Zoom). She needs a **Financial Brief Packet**.

### Your Mission: Assemble the Packet

1. **Executive summary** (1 page) - Problem, solution, ask amount
2. **Project budget** (Module 1, polished)
3. **Grant tracker** (Module 2, top 3 opportunities ranked)
4. **Mini-proposal** (Module 3, edited)

### Presentation Format

5-minute presentation as if you're briefing Mari: - 30 sec: Who is Oak Street - 90 sec: The funding need - 90 sec: Your recommended grant strategy - 60 sec: Timeline & next steps - 30 sec: What you need from her (signatures, data, stories)
