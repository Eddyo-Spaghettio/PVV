# MODULE 1: Two Doors of Funding
*"Same campus. Different keys."* - The Grant Whisperer

### 🎭 Story Hook: Indiana Jones and the Two Temples

Picture **Indiana Jones** standing before two doors:

🚪 **Door 1: The Stanford Temple** - Funding for *PVV* to operate (transportation to volunteer sites, club events, printing marketing materials). You apply as a **Stanford student service organization**. Money goes to PVV's accounts. Haas Center and OSE hold the keys.

🚪 **Door 2: The Community Temple** - Funding for *your client nonprofit* to run programs (tutoring, food, housing assistance). The **nonprofit** applies as a 501(c)(3) or community org. Money goes to *their* accounts. Foundations, government, and corporations hold the keys.

**Mixing these up is like using a Stanford ID to open a bank vault in East Palo Alto.** Wrong door. Wrong key. No money.

### Door 1: PVV / Stanford Funding (For the Club)

These fund **PVV's operations** - not your client's programs directly.

| Source | What It Covers | Amount | How to Apply | Who Applies |
|--------|---------------|--------|-------------|-------------|
| **Haas Center Transportation Grant** | Uber/Zipcar to volunteer sites | Up to $500/org/quarter | Haas Center portal; ONE app per SSO | PVV Treasurer |
| **Haas Center Event Co-Sponsorship** | PVV-hosted public service events | Varies | Haas Center resources page | PVV Treasurer + President |
| **OSE General Funding** | Student org programs/events | Varies | OSE funding portal | PVV Treasurer |
| **Department co-sponsorships** | Joint events with academic units | Varies | Direct outreach | PVV leadership |

**Key rules for PVV grants:** - Re-apply each quarter (transportation grants don't roll over) - Complete a projected budget BEFORE applying - Coordinate - only ONE transportation application per org per quarter - Submit reflection on prior grant impact when reapplying - Rides must be for public service activities only (not social outings)

**Example PVV budget line items:**

| Item | Amount | Justification |
|------|--------|---------------|
| Uber to Eastside Pantry volunteer day (12 students) | $180 | 4 trips × $45 avg |
| Printing 100 flyers for client event | $45 | FedEx Office quote attached |
| Canva Pro (1 month, if needed) | $13 | Marketing team deliverable |

### Door 2: Client Nonprofit Funding (For the Organization)

These fund **the nonprofit's mission work.** PVV helps write and organize - but the **nonprofit** is the applicant.

| Source Type | Examples | Typical Amount | Requirements |
|------------|----------|---------------|--------------|
| **Community foundations** | Silicon Valley Community Foundation | $5K–$50K+ | 501(c)(3), program narrative, budget |
| **Corporate giving** | Google.org, local banks, tech CSR | $1K–$25K | Alignment with company priorities |
| **Volunteer grants** | "Dollars for Doers" (employer matching) | $250–$1,000 | Volunteer hours logged by employees |
| **Government/CDBG** | City community development grants | Varies | Often requires detailed reporting |
| **Small family foundations** | Local donor-advised funds | $500–$5K | Shorter applications, relationship-based |

**Key rules for client grants:** - The **nonprofit's leadership** must sign and submit (you advise, they own it) - 501(c)(3) status is required for most grants (check each funder) - Budget numbers must match the narrative exactly - Funders want outcomes, not just activities ("50 students improved reading level" > "we tutored students") - Deadlines are immovable - start 4–6 weeks early

### The Funding Decision Tree

```
Is the money for PVV's own operations (transport, club events, supplies)?
  → YES → Door 1 (Stanford/Haas/OSE). Treasurer leads.
  → NO ↓

Is the money for the nonprofit's programs (staff, supplies, services)?
  → YES → Door 2 (Foundation/corporate/government). Financial team advises client.
  → NO ↓

Is it both (e.g., PVV event that also benefits client)?
  → Split the budget. PVV covers transportation; client covers program materials.
  → Be transparent with both funders about co-funding.
```

# MODULE 2: The Budget Blueprint
*"Funders don't fund vibes. They fund line items."* - The Grant Whisperer

### 🎭 Story Hook: The Breaking Bad Spreadsheet (Legal Edition)

**Walter White** was meticulous about his... inventory. Every gram accounted for. Every input tracked to an output.

Grant budgets work the same way (with fewer hazmat suits). Funders want to see:

1. **Every dollar has a job**
2. **Math that adds up**
3. **Realistic costs** (not "$1 for a building")
4. **Matching funds** if the grant requires them

### Budget Categories 101

| Category | What's Included | Example |
|----------|----------------|---------|
| **Personnel** | Salaries, stipends, payroll taxes | Part-time tutor @ $20/hr × 10 hrs/wk × 36 wks |
| **Direct Program** | Supplies, materials directly serving clients | Textbooks, food, art supplies |
| **Equipment** | Durable goods >$500 (sometimes) | Laptops, shelving, projector |
| **Transportation** | Travel to serve clients | Van rental, mileage reimbursement |
| **Admin/Operations** | Rent, utilities, insurance, office supplies | Often capped at 10–15% by funders |
| **Evaluation** | Surveys, data collection, reporting | Survey tools, external evaluator |
| **In-Kind/Match** | Donated goods/services (NOT cash from funder) | Volunteer hours valued at $29.95/hr (Independent Sector rate) |

### The Golden Rules of Grant Budgets

**Rule 1: Narrative ↔ Budget Mirror**
If your grant narrative says "We will serve 45 students with twice-weekly tutoring," your budget must show personnel hours and supplies for 45 students × 2 sessions/week. Reviewers check this.

**Rule 2: Round Numbers Are Suspicious**
"$10,000 for supplies" with no breakdown = instant skepticism.  
"Textbooks: 45 students × $35/book = $1,575" = credible.

**Rule 3: Admin Overhead Caps**
Many funders cap admin/indirect costs at 10–15%. Check the guidelines. If the cap is 10% on a $25,000 grant, max admin = $2,500.

**Rule 4: Show Match When Possible**
"Illustrating community support" - even in-kind volunteer hours - strengthens applications.

**Rule 5: Budget Notes Column Is Your Best Friend**
Every line item gets a note explaining the calculation. Future-you (and the funder) will thank you.

### Sample Budget: Bright Futures Youth Center

Open `templates/grant-budget-template.csv` - it's pre-filled with a realistic example. Study how each line item connects to a program activity.

**Narrative connection example:**

> *Grant narrative:* "Bright Futures provides twice-weekly after-school tutoring for 45 middle school students in East Palo Alto, focusing on math and reading remediation."
>
> *Budget proof:*
> - Tutoring Coordinator: 15 hrs/wk × 36 wks × $22/hr = $11,880
> - Textbooks/workbooks: 45 students × $40 = $1,800
> - Snacks: 45 students × 2 days/wk × 36 wks × $2/snack = $6,480
> - Background checks: 10 new volunteers × $35 = $350

# MODULE 3: The Grant Narrative Heist
*"Plan the proposal. Execute the proposal. Don't get rejected."* - Danny Ocean, Grant Edition

### 🎭 Story Hook: Ocean's Eleven, but Make It a Letter of Inquiry

Danny Ocean didn't walk into the vault and say "Give us money, we're cool." He had:

1. **A target** (which vault / which funder)
2. **A crew** (who does what)
3. **A timeline** (when each step happens)
4. **A story** (why this heist matters)
5. **An exit plan** (sustainability after the grant ends)

Grant applications follow the same structure.

### Anatomy of a Strong Grant Application

Most applications include these sections (names vary by funder):

| Section | What Funders Want | Length |
|---------|------------------|--------|
| **Organization Overview** | Who you are, mission, track record | 1 paragraph |
| **Problem Statement** | What community need exists (with data) | 1–2 paragraphs |
| **Program Description** | What you'll do, who you'll serve, how often | 2–3 paragraphs |
| **Goals & Outcomes** | SMART goals with measurable metrics | 1 page |
| **Evaluation Plan** | How you'll know it worked | 1 paragraph |
| **Budget + Budget Narrative** | Line items + explanation | Spreadsheet + 1 page |
| **Sustainability** | What happens when grant money runs out | 1 paragraph |
| **Attachments** | 501(c)(3) letter, board list, letters of support | Varies |

### SMART Goals Framework

| Letter | Meaning | Bad Example | Good Example |
|--------|---------|-------------|--------------|
| **S** | Specific | "Help students" | "Provide twice-weekly math tutoring" |
| **M** | Measurable | "Improve outcomes" | "80% of 45 students improve ≥1 grade level in math" |
| **A** | Achievable | "End poverty" | "Serve 45 students with existing staff capacity" |
| **R** | Relevant | Unrelated program | "Addresses 62% math proficiency gap in target school" |
| **T** | Time-bound | "Ongoing" | "By August 2026, measured via pre/post assessments" |

### The Problem Statement Formula

```
[DATA]: In [community], [X]% of [population] experience [problem].
[STORY]: For example, [one specific person/scenario - with permission].
[CONSEQUENCE]: Without intervention, [what happens].
[YOUR ANGLE]: [Organization] is uniquely positioned because [evidence].
```

**Example:**

> In East Palo Alto, 62% of 7th graders scored below grade level in math on the most recent state assessment (CA Dept of Education, 2025). Maria, a 12-year-old at Cesar Chavez Academy, told our staff she "stopped raising her hand in math class" after falling behind during COVID. Without targeted intervention, students like Maria face a 40% higher dropout risk (Stanford CEPA, 2024). Bright Futures has served this school for 6 years, maintaining an 85% program retention rate and partnerships with 3 local schools.

### Letter of Inquiry (LOI) - Your "Reconnaissance Mission"

Before a full application, many foundations want a **1–2 page LOI** to decide if you're invited to apply. Think of it as the **trailer** for your heist movie.

**LOI Template:**

```
[Date]

[Program Officer Name]
[Foundation Name]

Dear [Name],

INTRODUCTION (2–3 sentences)
[Bright Futures Youth Center] respectfully submits this Letter of Inquiry for 
[$25,000] to support [After-School Math & Reading Program] serving 
[45 low-income middle schoolers] in [East Palo Alto].

ORGANIZATION (1 paragraph)
[Who you are, years operating, key credentials, 501(c)(3) status]

NEED (1 paragraph)
[Problem statement with data + story]

PROPOSED PROGRAM (1 paragraph)
[What you'll do, timeline, key activities]

OUTCOMES (3–5 bullet points)
• [SMART outcome 1]
• [SMART outcome 2]
• [SMART outcome 3]

BUDGET SUMMARY (1 sentence)
Total project cost: $[X]. Requesting: $[Y]. Match/in-kind: $[Z].

CLOSING (2 sentences)
[Thank you + offer to provide full proposal upon invitation]

Sincerely,
[Executive Director Name]
[Title, Organization, Phone, Email]
```

### Common Rejection Reasons (And How to Avoid Them)

| Rejection Reason | Prevention |
|-----------------|-----------|
| Doesn't align with funder's priorities | Read their guidelines. Match their language. |
| Vague outcomes | Use SMART goals with numbers |
| Budget doesn't match narrative | Module 2 skills! Mirror test. |
| Missing 501(c)(3) documentation | Ask client for IRS determination letter early |
| Applied after deadline | Funding tracker with reminders |
| No evaluation plan | Include pre/post surveys, even free ones |
| Unrealistic scope | 45 students, not "all of East Palo Alto" |

### PVV's Role vs. Client's Role

| Task | PVV Financial Team | Client Nonprofit |
|------|-------------------|------------------|
| Research funders | ✅ Lead | Review + approve list |
| Draft narrative | ✅ Lead | Provide stories, data, edits |
| Build budget | ✅ Lead | Verify costs are realistic |
| Write LOI/proposal | ✅ Lead | Executive Director signs |
| Submit application | ❌ Advise only | ✅ Client submits |
| Report on grant | ❌ Support | ✅ Client reports to funder |
| Maintain records | ✅ Help organize | ✅ Client owns files |

**You are consultants, not the applicant.** The client's name goes on the form.

# 📚 Quick Reference: Financial Team Do's and Don'ts

### ✅ Do - Start early (6 weeks before deadline) - Match funder language in your narrative - Keep copies of everything in a shared PVV Drive folder - Loop in the PVV Treasurer for Door 1 (club) grants - Verify all numbers with the client ("Is $22/hr realistic for your coordinator?") - Include an evaluation plan in every proposal

### ❌ Don't - Submit a grant application on behalf of a client without their ED's signature - Inflate budget numbers to "look bigger" - Confuse PVV funding with client funding on forms - Promise a client they'll "definitely get the grant" - Forget sustainability ("What happens after the money runs out?") - Use outdated data (check sources are ≤2 years old)
