# MODULE 1: The Strategy Scroll
*"Know thy audience before thy post."* - Marketing Yoda

### 🎭 Story Hook: The Infinity Post Gauntlet

Imagine **Tony Stark** trying to promote the Avengers by posting on every platform simultaneously - TikTok dances, LinkedIn thought leadership, a Pinterest mood board, and a MySpace page. Chaos. Burnout. Zero engagement.

**Thor** would say: "This is not worthy."

Marketing for small nonprofits works the same way. **One clear goal. One primary audience. One or two channels.** That's the whole gauntlet.

### Step 1: Pick ONE Goal (Not Five)

For the next 90 days, your client gets **one primary outcome**:

| Goal | Example Metric | Good For |
|------|---------------|----------|
| Event attendance | 100 RSVPs | Fundraisers, volunteer days |
| Volunteer recruitment | 20 new sign-ups | Ongoing programs |
| Donations | $2,000 raised | Giving campaigns |
| Awareness | 500 new followers OR 10K impressions | New orgs, rebrands |

**❌ Bad goal:** "Increase awareness, get donations, recruit volunteers, and go viral."  
**✅ Good goal:** "Get 150 people to our March 15 Taco Tuesday fundraiser."

### Step 2: Know Your Audience (Not "Everyone")

Use this **Audience Avatar** worksheet:

```
NAME: "Community Carla" (made up, but based on real data)
AGE: 28-45
LIVES: East Palo Alto / nearby
CARES ABOUT: Feeding her kids, community events, free/low-cost activities
SCROLLS: Instagram in the evening, checks Facebook events on weekends
WON'T: Read a 12-paragraph caption or click a broken link
NEEDS FROM YOU: Clear date, location, what to bring, why it matters
```

For Stanford outreach, create a second avatar: **"Service Sam"** - junior, wants meaningful volunteering, discovers events through Haas Center emails and Instagram.

### Step 3: Choose Your Channels

**Rule of thumb for PVV clients:**

| Channel | Best For | Time Investment |
|---------|----------|-----------------|
| Instagram | Visual storytelling, events, younger audiences | Medium |
| Facebook | Community groups, event pages, older demographics | Medium |
| Printed flyers | Local businesses, laundromats, churches, Haas Center | Low (one-time design) |
| Haas Center promotion | Stanford student volunteers + attendees | Low (one email/blurb) |
| Email | Existing supporters, donors, volunteers | Low if list exists |

**Pick 1 primary + 1 secondary.** Master those before adding TikTok.

### Step 4: Build Content Pillars (Your Greatest Hits Album)

Create **3–4 repeatable content types** you rotate through:

| Pillar | Example Post | Why It Works |
|--------|-------------|--------------|
| **Impact Story** | "Meet Maria - she picked up groceries for her family of 5 today" | Emotion → action |
| **Behind the Scenes** | Volunteers sorting produce at 6 AM | Humanizes the org |
| **Event Promo** | Taco Tuesday countdown graphic | Drives attendance |
| **Education** | "1 in 5 neighbors in our zip code faces food insecurity" | Builds urgency + shares |

You're not inventing content from scratch every Tuesday. You're remixing the album.

### Step 5: Write Your Key Message

Fill in this mad-lib:

> **[Organization]** helps **[who]** by **[how]**. Right now, we need **[specific ask]** so that **[impact]**.

**Example:**
> *Eastside Community Pantry helps low-income families in East Palo Alto by providing free weekly groceries. Right now, we need you at our Taco Tuesday fundraiser on March 15 so that we can keep our shelves stocked through summer.*

Every post, flyer, and caption should echo some version of this.

# MODULE 2: The Poster Prophecy
*"A picture is worth a thousand RSVPs."* - Someone at Canva

### 🎭 Story Hook: The Fellowship of the Flyer

**Frodo** didn't walk to Mordor with a vague scroll. He had a **clear map, a deadline, and friends who knew the way.**

Your poster is that map. In 3 seconds, a stranger walking past a laundromat bulletin board must understand:

1. **WHAT** is happening (Taco Tuesday Fundraiser)
2. **WHEN** (March 15, 5–8 PM)
3. **WHERE** (East Palo Alto Community Center + address)
4. **WHY** they should care (Supports 200 local families)
5. **WHAT TO DO** (Show up / scan QR code / DM for info)

If any of those are missing, the poster goes to the **Shadow Realm** (recycling bin).

### Poster Design Rules (No Design Degree Needed)

**The Hierarchy of Importance:**

```
1. EVENT NAME - biggest text, readable from 10 feet
2. DATE + TIME - second biggest
3. LOCATION - include address
4. ONE compelling line about impact
5. Logo + contact/QR code - smallest but present
```

**Color psychology (quick cheat sheet):** - 🟢 Green = community, growth, food/nature (great for pantries!) - 🔵 Blue = trust, stability - 🟠 Orange/Yellow = energy, urgency, fun (great for fundraisers) - ⚫ Too much text = people walk faster

**Free tools PVV recommends:** - [Canva](https://canva.com) - templates galore, free tier is plenty - [Adobe Express](https://express.adobe.com) - quick social resizing - Google Slides - underrated, collaborative, free

### The "Squint Test"

Print or zoom out until the poster is thumbnail-sized. Can you still read the event name and date? If not, simplify.

### Flyer Distribution Strategy

A beautiful poster in zero locations = a tree falling in an empty forest.

**Stanford-specific locations:** - Haas Center bulletin boards + digital newsletter - Community centers near client site - Relevant student group mailing lists (with permission)

**Community locations:** - Laundromats, barber shops, bodegas, church lobbies - Library community boards - Partner org offices

**Always ask permission** before posting. Ms. Elena will often know which businesses say yes.

# MODULE 3: The Viral-ish Launch
*"We're not going viral. We're going *visible*."* - Realistic Marketing Hero

### 🎭 Story Hook: The Ocean's Eleven Content Heist

**Danny Ocean** didn't rob the casino with one move. He had a **timeline, roles, and a sequence of perfectly timed actions.**

Your content launch works the same way:

| Week | Heist Move | Your Content Move |
|------|-----------|-------------------|
| Week -2 | Reconnaissance | Teaser post: "Something big is coming..." |
| Week -1 | Assemble the crew | Event details drop + flyer reveal |
| Week -0 | Distraction | Daily countdown stories |
| Event day | The heist | Live stories, photos, "We're here!" |
| Week +1 | Split the loot | Thank-you post + impact numbers |

### The 2-Week Content Calendar

Open `templates/marketing-content-calendar.csv` - it's pre-filled with a sample plan. Customize it for your client.

**Posting cadence for small nonprofits:** 2–3 quality posts per week beats daily mediocre posts.

### Caption Formula (Copy-Paste Friendly)

```
[HOOK - question, stat, or bold statement]

[STORY - 2-3 sentences about why this matters]

[DETAILS - date, time, location, link]

[CTA - one clear action: "Link in bio to RSVP" or "Share with a friend"]

[HASHTAGS - 5-10 relevant, not 30 random ones]
```

**Example caption:**

> 200 families. One community. Zero reason to miss this. 🌮
>
> Eastside Community Pantry has kept our neighbors fed every week for 8 years - but summer is our hardest season. Join us for Taco Tuesday on March 15 (5–8 PM) at the East Palo Alto Community Center. Every taco purchased keeps our shelves stocked for another day.
>
> 📍 1530 Bay Rd, East Palo Alto  
> 🔗 RSVP link in bio  
> 💚 Bring a friend. Bring your appetite. Bring the vibes.
>
> #EastPaloAlto #CommunityPantry #TacoTuesday #BayAreaNonprofit #FoodJustice

### Engagement Strategy (The Other Half of Posting)

Posting is half the job. **Engagement is the other half:** - Reply to every comment in the first hour - Share user-generated content (event photos from attendees) - Thank donors/volunteers by name (with permission) - Cross-post to relevant Facebook community groups (with admin approval)

### Metrics That Matter

Ignore vanity metrics early on. Track what connects to your **one goal**:

| Goal | Track This | Ignore This (For Now) |
|------|-----------|----------------------|
| Event attendance | RSVPs, check-ins, "How did you hear about us?" | Total followers |
| Donations | $ raised, donation link clicks | Likes on unrelated posts |
| Volunteers | Form submissions, email sign-ups | Impressions without action |

**Monthly report template (1 page):**
1. Primary metric result vs. goal
2. One story of impact ("Maria came because she saw our Instagram post")
3. What worked / what didn't
4. Plan for next month

# 📚 Quick Reference: PVV Marketing Do's and Don'ts

### ✅ Do - Get client approval before posting anything - Use the client's logo, colors, and voice (not yours) - Include accessibility basics (alt text on images, readable fonts) - Save all files in a shared PVV Google Drive folder - Document what you did so the next team can continue

### ❌ Don't - Post personal opinions on the client's account - Use copyrighted music in Reels without checking licenses - Promise results you can't measure ("We'll go viral!") - Abandon an account after your quarter ends - train the client or next PVV member - Forget to credit photographers and volunteers in posts
