# Bridge Kit | Youth Engagement Proposal

## The Problem (Briefly)

Our partner nonprofits need younger volunteers and donors. They also need those people to come back.

This isn't because young people don't care. Blackbaud's Gen Z philanthropy research (2024) found 84% of Gen Z supports causes in some form: volunteering, donating, sharing, advocating. Candid's analysis adds that Gen Z is 10× more likely than boomers to share their support on social media.

The gap is on the nonprofit side:

1. **Discovery** - students don't know the org exists
2. **Friction** - sign-up requires a phone call, paper form, or broken website
3. **Social proof** - nothing shows peers already involved
4. **Return loop** - no reason to come back after one event

Our partners, often led by experienced directors in their 50s–60s who've built trust in their communities for decades, typically can't fix this alone. Research on digital literacy for older adults consistently shows they need ongoing, hands-on help, not a new tool dropped in their lap. They're excellent at mission and community relationships. They're not on Instagram.

PVV's unique position: we have the digital skills AND access to the Stanford student population. No SaaS product replicates that.

---

## Why Not an "Ad Generator"

An automatic ad/video generator sounds appealing but fails our constraints:

| Issue | Reality |
|-------|---------|
| Cost | Video AI tools cost money; quality is inconsistent |
| Maintenance | Requires someone to keep updating it during the school year |
| Client usability | Older directors won't log into a generator, pick templates, and publish. They need finished assets and a person who helps. |
| Missing the actual problem | The bottleneck isn't content creation. It's **distribution to Stanford students** and **low-friction sign-up** |

What works better: a **standardized deliverable package** that PVV consultants build for each client, plus an **Assigned Liaison** who operates the parts the director can't.

---

## The Proposal: Bridge Kit

**Bridge Kit** is a free, repeatable package PVV delivers to every partner nonprofit. It's not software. It's a set of concrete assets + a person, designed to connect older-led community orgs with younger volunteers and donors.

Think of it as the thing PVV is known for, like how SCNO chapters are known for 10-week consulting engagements, but ours is specifically built for the youth engagement gap.

### What's in the Kit

| Component | What It Is | Who Builds It | Who Maintains It |
|-----------|-----------|---------------|-----------------|
| **1. Reach Page** | One mobile-friendly landing page: volunteer sign-up, donate link, social links, 2-sentence mission | PVV Technical team | Assigned Liaison (Quarter 1); client can request updates anytime |
| **2. QR Flyer** | Print-ready flyer with QR code → Reach Page. For laundromats, churches, Haas Center, client lobby | PVV Marketing team | Client reprints as needed (same file) |
| **3. Stanford Channel Pack** | Pre-written blurb for Haas service4all listserv, CardinalEngage event post, email to student orgs | PVV Outreach | PVV posts each event; template reusable |
| **4. 90-Day Content Calendar** | 12 weeks of pre-written social posts (copy + what photo to use). Not a generator: a schedule with drafts | PVV Marketing team | Assigned Liaison posts; client approves once at kickoff |
| **5. Assigned Liaison** | One PVV member assigned to the client for the quarter. Recruits Stanford peers, posts on personal account, attends one client event | PVV Outreach/Marketing | Rotates each quarter with documented handoff |

### Why Each Piece Exists

**Reach Page** - Gen Z research shows spontaneous, mobile giving and sign-up. If the path from "I saw this" to "I'm signed up" takes more than 2 minutes or requires a desktop, you lose them. One page, three buttons.

**QR Flyer** - Our clients' communities still discover things offline (churches, food pantries, community boards). The QR bridges physical → digital without the director learning web design.

**Stanford Channel Pack** - This is PVV's actual moat. Haas service4all, Cardinal Service Fair, and student org partnerships are channels no marketing tool can access. We ARE the distribution.

**90-Day Calendar** - Nonprofits don't fail because they can't write one post. They fail because they don't post consistently. A calendar with drafts removes the blank-page problem. The Assigned Liaison adapts and posts.

**Assigned Liaison** - Solves the tech gap human-to-human. The director provides stories and approval. The student handles Instagram, peer recruitment, and Stanford outreach. This mirrors intergenerational models (Encore.org's Gen2Gen, AARP/Red Cross youth programs) that pair younger and older collaborators rather than expecting one person to do everything.

## Minimal Work for Clients (Replace, Don't Add)

Nonprofits told us they want **as little extra work as possible**. Bridge Kit is built around that.

### When it replaces work they already do

| What they already struggle with | What Bridge Kit replaces |
|--------------------------------|--------------------------|
| Updating a volunteer flyer or word-of-mouth sign-up | Reach Page + QR flyer (one link, works on phones) |
| Writing social posts from scratch every week | 90-day calendar with drafts ready to post |
| Emailing Stanford groups one-by-one | Stanford Channel Pack (copy-paste blurb) |
| Chasing student volunteers through personal networks | Assigned Liaison recruits peers and posts where students actually are |

### When it doesn't replace existing work

Some orgs have almost no digital presence today. Bridge Kit still doesn't add a new job for the director: - **PVV builds finished assets** (not "here's a tool, figure it out") - **Assigned Liaison owns posting and Stanford outreach** for 90 days - **Client role:** one kickoff call (~45 min), approve copy/photos once, optionally share 2–3 photos from programs they already run

**Realistic client time:** ~2 hours at kickoff, then ~15 minutes/week only if they want to review posts before they go live. Many clients approve the full calendar upfront and don't touch it again.

## Long-Term PVV Involvement (Sustainable)

PVV can stay involved without burning out officers or creating dependency.

| Phase | PVV effort | Client effort | What persists |
|-------|-----------|---------------|---------------|
| **Quarter 1 (delivery)** | ~15–20 hrs total across team; liaison ~2–3 hrs/week | ~2 hrs kickoff + optional photo shares | Reach Page, QR file, calendar, Stanford templates |
| **After 90 days (handoff)** | 30-min check-in; liaison writes handoff notes in Console/Drive | None required | Client keeps using Reach Page link on existing flyers, email sigs, etc. |
| **Ongoing (each new quarter)** | New Assigned Liaison: ~1 hr/week per active client (check metrics, post calendar, one Stanford blast) | Optional 15-min call if content needs refresh | Same Reach Page unless org info changes |
| **Annual refresh** | One technical session to update links, stats, photos | Approve changes once | Updated kit for next year |

**Why this scales for PVV:**

1. **Documented handoffs** - Console exports + Drive notes mean a new liaison picks up in 30 minutes, not from scratch
2. **Same 5 components every time** - training is repeatable; HTML workbooks cover it
3. **GitHub + JSON** - Reach Page updates are a commit, not a rebuild from zero
4. **Clear scope at kickoff** - "90 days active liaison support, then lightweight quarterly check-ins" prevents "run our Instagram forever" expectations
5. **Officer turnover** - spring shadow period + exported kit JSON means next year's PVV doesn't lose client relationships

If a client goes quiet after handoff, they still keep the Reach Page and QR flyer with zero maintenance. PVV doesn't owe infinite labor; the assets keep working.

## How It's Different From What PVV Already Does

PVV currently offers open-ended consulting (websites, grants, social media). That's valuable but inconsistent: deliverables vary by team, handoffs are unclear, and clients don't know what to expect.

Bridge Kit standardizes the **core package** while leaving room for additional consulting:

```
Every client gets:     Bridge Kit (5 components above)
Optional add-ons:      Full website rebuild, grant writing, data pipeline, budget overhaul
```

This gives PVV a clear identity: *"We're the Stanford group that helps local nonprofits reach young people."*

## Who It's For

**Primary clients:** Small CBOs ($200K–$2M budget) serving low-income communities in San Mateo County, especially EPA and Redwood City. Often led by directors who've been in the community 10+ years. 1–3 paid staff. Volunteers are the operating engine.

**Examples:** EHP, St. Francis Center, Nuestra Casa, local food pantries, legal aid clinics, youth tutoring programs.

**Not for:** Large foundations, national orgs with marketing departments, or orgs that already have a strong youth volunteer pipeline.

## Implementation Plan

### Phase 1: Pilot (One Quarter) - Pick 1 existing partner (Rosalie Rendu Center or St. Francis Center) - Deliver full Bridge Kit - Name the Assigned Liaison - Track: page visits, form submissions, event attendance from Stanford, social engagement - 30-day and 90-day check-in with client director

### Phase 2: Standardize (Two Quarters) - Refine templates based on pilot feedback - Train all three consulting tracks on Bridge Kit components - Deliver to 2–3 clients simultaneously - Document everything in shared Drive

### Phase 3: Scale (Ongoing) - Every new client starts with Bridge Kit - Assigned Liaison becomes a defined PVV role (like CCC but client-facing) - Collect impact stories for recruitment and Haas reports - Optional: present at Haas workshop as a model for other SSOs

## Risks and Mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
|------|-----------|
| Client doesn't post the content calendar | Assigned Liaison owns posting; director approves once at kickoff |
| Assigned Liaison disappears mid-quarter | Backup liaison named at kickoff; everything documented in Console/Drive |
| Reach Page goes unmaintained after PVV leaves | Handoff doc + 30-day check-in; page stays live with no client action required |
| Client expects PVV to run their social forever | Scope doc at kickoff: 90 days active support, then quarterly light touch |
| Bridge Kit feels like "more work" for the client | Kickoff framing: we replace flyer/sign-up/outreach tasks; liaison executes |
| Low Stanford turnout | Post on service4all + partner with 1 student org + free food at event |

## Why This Could Work Long-Term

1. **Repeatable** - same 5 components every time, customized per client
2. **Documented** - templates in Drive, training in HTML workbooks, JSON in GitHub
3. **Human-centered** - respects that our clients are older leaders who shouldn't be expected to become social media managers
4. **Uses PVV's actual advantage** - Stanford access, not technology
5. **Free** - no budget barrier for the orgs that need it most
6. **Measurable** - form submissions and event attendance are concrete
7. **Survives turnover** - if templates and handoff docs exist, next year's PVV picks up where you left off
8. **Low client burden** - replaces existing pain points; liaison absorbs ongoing execution
