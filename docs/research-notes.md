# Research Notes - What We Found and Why It Matters

Quick reference for PVV leadership. Sources linked where possible.

---

## 1. The Youth Engagement Gap Is Real - But It's Not Apathy

**What the data says:** - 84% of Gen Z supports nonprofits in some way (Blackbaud Institute, 2024) - Gen Z is 10× more likely than boomers to share donations on social media (Candid, 2024) - Gen Z prefers volunteering before donating - 61% volunteer at least once/year; financial giving often follows (Boston College CCC, 2024) - Gen Z wants data-driven impact stories, not promotional fluff (Nonprofit Management & Leadership journal, 2024) - The Red Cross/DoSomething "Three C's" framework: young people want **Community impact, Connections, and Career relevance** (2024)

**What this means for PVV:**
Young people aren't the problem. The problem is that our clients' outreach doesn't meet Gen Z where they are - mobile, social, peer-driven, low-commitment first. Bridge Kit is designed around these findings, not around guessing.

---

## 2. Bay Area Nonprofit Leaders Are Experienced - and Stretched Thin

**What the data says:** - Bay Area is seeing a wave of executive director transitions as long-tenured leaders retire (Bay Area Interim Executive Directors network) - Small org EDs (<$2M budget) in SF Bay Area earn $105K–$145K - they're professionals, not volunteers, but their orgs run lean (ExecSearches.com, 2026) - Fewer than 20% of leaders at sub-$1M nonprofits access professional coaching (CNPC, 2026) - BIPOC leaders face structural barriers to advancement despite higher leadership aspirations (Building Movement Project, Race to Lead 2024)

**What this means for PVV:**
Our partners are competent, committed leaders running understaffed orgs. They don't need us to tell them how to run programs. They need capacity - especially digital and youth-facing capacity they can't hire for. Respect their expertise. Don't condescend.

---

## 3. Older Adults Struggle With Tech - and That's Normal

**What the data says:** - Digital literacy for older adults requires ongoing, patient, hands-on support - not one-time training (LeadingAge, OATS/Senior Planet, 2024) - Fear, anxiety, and embarrassment are major barriers - not just skill gaps (Belonging Forum scoping review, 2025) - Intergenerational tech programs (younger volunteers helping older adults) are among the most effective models (2Life Communities case study, LeadingAge) - Asset-based framing works better than deficit framing - "digital skills training" not "you can't use technology"

**What this means for PVV:**
Don't build tools clients must operate alone. Build finished assets + assign a student liaison. The intergenerational model IS our model. Frame it as collaboration, not charity.

---

## 4. Intergenerational Models Work - When They're Intentional

**What the data says:** - Gen2Gen/Encore.org: civic matchmaking between older volunteers and orgs needing capacity is proven (Gen2Gen Cities report) - Shared leadership across generations requires intentional recruitment, clear pathways, and genuine influence - not token youth advisory boards (Annie E. Casey Foundation toolkit, 2026) - Older volunteers stay longer once engaged; younger volunteers need flexible, short-term entry points (Gen2Gen Cities)

**What this means for PVV:**
Bridge Kit's Assigned Liaison role is an intergenerational design. The client director provides wisdom and stories; the student provides digital reach and peer recruitment. Both sides contribute something the other can't.

---

## 5. Student Consulting Clubs That Last Have Repeatable Models

**What the data says:** - SCNO (20+ chapters): standardized 10-week project cycle with professional mentors - Penn SIC: 100+ clients since 2007 - clear client intake and project scope - Pitt Incline: teams of 5–6, 12-week deliverables, diverse academic backgrounds - Common success factors: defined project length, written deliverables, client collaboration throughout, alumni network

**What this means for PVV:**
Open-ended consulting burns out members and confuses clients. Bridge Kit gives us the SCNO-style repeatable model. Additional consulting is the upsell, not the default.

## 6. Stanford Has More Resources Than Most Clubs Use

**Key findings from Haas + OSE:**

| Resource | Most clubs underuse it because... |
|----------|----------------------------------|
| Haas advising | They don't know it's available for strategy, not just logistics |
| service4all listserv | They forget to post client events |
| Custom Haas training (10+ people) | They don't ask |
| Transportation grants | They miss the quarterly reapplication window |
| TSF Partnership Program | Seems complicated - but $5K is significant if you're established |
| Haas office/craft supplies | Free flyer materials sitting at Haas |

**What this means for PVV:**
The operations plan's contact list and quarterly calendar exist because clubs die when treasurers don't reapply for Haas transport grants in Week 10 of the prior quarter. Set calendar reminders now.

## 9. Local Context Worth Knowing

**East Palo Alto:** - Median income well below San Mateo County average - Strong existing CBO network (EHP, Nuestra Casa, YUCA, EPA CAN DO) - City publishes a housing/resource guide listing dozens of orgs - useful for partner research - Many residents are Spanish-speaking - materials in English and Spanish when possible

**Redwood City / North Fair Oaks:** - St. Francis Center is a hub org (food, housing, youth) - Fair Oaks Community Center coordinates referrals - Growing population, increasing service demand

**Stanford's relationship to these communities:** - Physically close but socially disconnected - students often don't know these orgs exist - Haas transportation grants exist specifically to bridge this distance - Cardinal Service requirements create a pool of students looking for meaningful engagement
