# PVV Operations Plan - 2026–27

This is the practical guide for keeping Proyecto Vidas Valiosas running: who to contact, what Stanford resources to use, how money flows, and how work gets done without depending on any one person or tool.

---

## What PVV Actually Is

PVV connects Stanford students with small community nonprofits in the Bay Area - mostly food pantries, housing orgs, legal aid, and youth programs serving low-income neighborhoods in East Palo Alto, Redwood City, and nearby cities.

**Our edge:** students who can build websites, write grants, run social media, and - critically - bring other Stanford students as volunteers and donors. Most of our partner orgs cannot do that last part on their own.

**Our constraint:** we're students. Leadership turns over every year. Nothing should live in one person's head or one paid tool.

---

## The Problem We're Solving (For Clients)

Research consistently shows Gen Z already gives and volunteers at high rates - but not through traditional nonprofit channels. They want: - Low-friction sign-up (mobile, under 2 minutes) - Visible impact with real numbers - Peer social proof (seeing people their age involved) - Flexible time commitments, not open-ended weekly shifts - Opportunities tied to community and career growth (Red Cross/DoSomething "Three C's": Community, Connections, Careers)

Meanwhile, our partner nonprofits are often led by directors in their 40s–60s who've run programs for decades but struggle with Instagram, outdated websites, and reaching anyone under 25. Digital literacy research shows older adults need **ongoing, patient, hands-on support** - not a one-time training or a tool they're expected to figure out alone.

PVV sits in the gap between these two groups. That's the whole point.

## Yearly Rhythm

| Quarter | Focus | Key Actions |
|---------|-------|-------------|
| **Autumn** | Recruit + onboard + match clients | Info session, training workbooks, partner intake, assign Bridge Kits |
| **Winter** | Deliver + first client check-ins | Project sprints, Haas transportation grants, mid-quarter client calls |
| **Spring** | Finish deliverables + transition | Client handoffs, officer elections (by Apr 15), shadow period for incoming board |
| **Summer** | Light ops / planning | Outgoing officers document lessons; incoming officers read ops plan |

**Weekly cadence (during school year):** - 1 general member meeting (project updates, training module, community) - 1 operations meeting (officers + project leads only) - Monthly client check-in per active partnership (15 min, phone or Zoom)

## Stanford Resources - What to Use and When

### Haas Center for Public Service (primary partner)

**Contact:** Geoff Baker - glbaker@stanford.edu (SSO resources, space, transportation, advising)

| Resource | Use For | Details |
|----------|---------|---------|
| **Advising** | Strategy, partnerships, ethical service | Book via Haas SEL team. Use for stuck partnerships, scope issues, or "are we doing this right?" |
| **Transportation grants** | Getting students to client sites | Up to $500/org/quarter. ONE application per org. Reapply each quarter with impact reflection. Opens last week of prior quarter. |
| **Event co-sponsorship** | PVV events over $500 need | Under $500, request 30+ days ahead. Must align with public service mission. |
| **Meeting + storage space** | Weekly meetings, supply storage | Request recurring space Wks 2–10 each quarter. Storage is limited - don't hoard. |
| **service4all listserv** | Promoting client events to Stanford | Email list for service opportunities. Use for every client event worth student attendance. |
| **Cardinal Service Fair** | Recruiting PVV members + promoting clients | Haas-run. Show up with client materials. |
| **Custom training** | Onboarding 10+ members at once | Request workshops on facilitation, community partnerships, leadership transitions, measuring impact. |
| **Canvas: Ethical & Effective Service** | Required baseline for all members | Enroll every new member. Link in onboarding. |
| **Office/craft supplies + button maker** | Flyers, event materials | Free supplies at Haas. Button maker for promo (up to 50 buttons). |
| **Copiers (CardinalPrint)** | Printing client materials | Haas workrooms, first and second floor. |

**Why Haas first:** They're built for exactly what PVV does. Transportation and promotion are our two biggest operational needs. Don't duplicate what they already offer.

### Office of Student Engagement (OSE)

**Contact:** studentengagement@stanford.edu | Drop-in office hours + workshops

| Resource | Use For | Details |
|----------|---------|---------|
| **CardinalEngage** | Membership, events, org management | Required. All members must register. Post events here. |
| **ASSU Quick Grants** | Fast funding for specific events | Fastest option if you need money quickly for a one-off. |
| **ASSU Annual Grants** | Planned yearly programming | Apply if you can predict budget a quarter ahead. |
| **The Stanford Fund (TSF)** | Stable supplemental funding | Up to $5,000 request. Requires STARS training for FO. Spring + Autumn application cycles. Must do donor engagement activities in return. Good if PVV is established with regular meetings and leadership transitions. |
| **Corporate Fundraising Program** | Company donations to PVV (not clients) | Apply Spring for following AY. Mandatory training (May). Faculty advisor approval required starting 2026–27. |
| **Working with Minors policy** | Any project involving under-18 clients | Background checks (Live Scan) + training required. Plan 4–6 weeks ahead. |

**Funding strategy:** Haas for service-specific costs (transport). ASSU Quick for emergencies. TSF for stable annual budget if you're ready for the reporting/engagement requirements. Never solicit alumni directly - OSE/Development handles that.

## Money: How It Flows

### PVV's costs (typical annual budget)

| Item | Est. Cost | Source |
|------|-----------|--------|
| Transportation to client sites | $1,500–2,000/yr | Haas transportation grants |
| Printing (client flyers, materials) | $200–400/yr | Haas co-sponsorship or ASSU Quick |
| Canva/subscriptions (if any) | $0–150/yr | Free tiers usually sufficient |
| Event food/supplies | $300–500/yr | Haas co-sponsorship |
| **Total** | **~$2,000–3,000/yr** | Mostly Haas + ASSU |

### What PVV does NOT fund - Client program costs (that's the client's job - we help them find grants) - Paid software for clients (use free tools: Google Sites, Forms, Canva free, Givebutter) - Anything requiring ongoing subscription maintenance clients can't afford

## Sustainability Checklist

What keeps PVV alive year after year: - [ ] **Repeatable deliverable** (Bridge Kit) - every client gets the same core package, customized - [ ] **Documented training** (HTML workbooks) - new members self-onboard without a senior present - [ ] **Quarterly Haas grant cycle** - treasurer calendar reminder, not ad hoc - [ ] **2+ multi-year client relationships** - proof of value, easier recruitment - [ ] **Spring shadow period** - incoming officers work alongside outgoing for 4–6 weeks - [ ] **Client handoff docs** - orgs can maintain tools after PVV leaves - [ ] **Low tool dependency** - Google Workspace + Canva + free platforms only

## Metrics That Matter

Track these quarterly. Ignore vanity numbers.

| Metric | Target | Why |
|--------|--------|-----|
| Active client partnerships | 3–5 | Quality over quantity |
| Bridge Kits delivered | 1 per client per quarter | Core product |
| Stanford students reached via promotion | 200+ per event | Distribution value |
| Client-reported volunteer/donor increase | Any measurable uptick | Actual impact |
| Member retention (return next quarter) | >50% | Club health |
| Deliverables with handoff docs | 100% | Sustainability |
